# M1 literature review summary

**Status:** Targeted review complete for the acquisition decision and extended on 2026-10-08 to check public official guidance on saturation, dynamic-range, and fill semantics. Literature and official mode validity identify E1G2 as a candidate thermal mode. Its matched scene is acquired, but the full-cube audit failed the saturation-threshold gate, so E1G2 is not yet a validated processing input.

**Scope:** Selected Chandrayaan-2 IIRS radiance, thermal correction, reflectance, and photometric correction studies. This is an initial targeted review, not a systematic review.

**Sources:** Primary literature from 2020–2026, including a 2025 LPSC abstract on raw-count recalibration; the official PRADAN IIRS FAQ; ISRO's public Chandrayaan-2 processing description; the local official SIS, calibration archive, and band-validity workbook; and a public GPL-3.0 processing repository.

**Method:** Search paper abstracts and publisher/ISRO pages for IIRS thermal correction, E1G2 use, reflectance retrieval, and validation. Inspect publisher article content for mode, wavelength, and sensitivity details. Compare reported wavelength ranges with the local official mode-validity CSV.

**Main observation:** The 2022 thermal-correction study uses E1G2 and 2.5–5 µm. The local official workbook marks E1G2 bands 226–248 (4504.1–4874.9 nm) valid. A 2025 LPSC abstract uses the ISRO LUT, saturation radiance, and CCD dynamic range to convert linearized raw counts to radiance. Its reconstructed radiance differs from the ISRO Level-1 product, partly because the authors did not apply order-sorting-filter adjustments or keystone correction. This supports reviewing the fields as calibration inputs, but does not establish the official table's exact semantics or a pixel mask. The papers reviewed do not define IIRS fill values or the calibrated cube's invalid-pixel encoding. A 2026 study reports strong reflectance sensitivity to temperature error. E1G2 remains a supported acquisition target, not a validated correction input.

**Limitations:** The literature review did not resolve saturation-table semantics or cube fill rules, provide independent radiometric validation, or validate a thermal correction on local data. The 2025 LPSC result is a conference abstract, not a detailed calibration specification. It does not publish a fill convention or per-pixel saturation rule. A subsequent authenticated PRADAN selection produced a matched E1G2 pair, documented in the [acquisition record](../m1-e1g2-scene/summary.md). A live read-only check of the public PRADAN browse route redirected to sign-in; the scene table is not publicly visible without authentication. Some full texts are not openly available. The paper-index search had weak coverage for this specialized planetary-science literature, so Exa searches and publisher, conference, and official pages were also checked.

**2026-10-08 public-source check:** The PRADAN FAQ directs users to the IIRS SIS and guide but does not state pixel mask or fill rules. ISRO's public mission processing description calls Level 1 a radiometrically corrected, geometrically tagged radiance product; it does not specify these rules. This search did not close the gate. The LPSC 2025 abstract remains a method lead for raw-count recalibration, not an authority for Level-1 masks.

**Next gate:** Ask the IIRS team to confirm how the saturation-radiance and dynamic-range fields apply to Level-1 pixels, and to provide the cube fill and validity rules. Then validate radiance scale independently and review full-resolution geometry. Only after those gates pass should the published thermal method be tested against independent temperature and reflectance references.
