# Findings

- **The 1 µm band-depth map is the most informative parameter.** It is highest in the dark units: the Sinus Medii edge (about 1–4.5°N), the Rimae Bode dark mantle deposit (about 9.5–14°N), and the plains at about 19–22.5°N. This is consistent with Fe-bearing mafic or glass-rich material. It is not a mineral identification.
- **Failed gate: geographic stability of spectral units.** Block-split ARI is 0.60 or less for every k, against a target of 0.8. Seed stability passes (0.95 or more for k = 3–6). The units are reproducible but not region-invariant. Do not present them as a fixed geologic map.
- **Failed gate: IBD 2 µm signal-to-noise ratio.** Its pixel-scale correlation with IBD 1 µm is r = 0.02. For pyroxene the two bands should co-vary, so the 2 µm parameter is dominated by noise or by artifacts at this resolution. Spatial binning may help.
- **Detector artifacts handled.** Despiking replaced 1.6% of band values in the 845–2460 nm window. An intermittently bad detector element near sample 155 at 845–880 nm produced an empty "unit" before edge bands were despiked. Junction steps (0.95–1.05 polishing range) are removed only as a scene-common factor.
- **Early iterations recorded.** A 25% scene-shape screen removed 62% of pixels. A 6-sigma screen with column removal at more than 10% failures dropped 85 columns. Both were rejected as too aggressive before the replace-and-keep despiking rule was adopted. The parameters were chosen on visual and stability grounds. No label score was used.
- **Binning helps only partly (2026-10-10).** 4×4 binning raises the best block-split ARI from 0.60 to 0.71. The gate still fails. Alternate 500-line blocks contain different mixes of terrain, so k-means trained on each half finds different centroids. The units are therefore scene-composition dependent. A supervised model with independent labels is the better M3 test. The target was not lowered after seeing results.
