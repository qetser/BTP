# E3G2 paired raw–LUT–calibrated audit

**Status:** Full valid-band audit completed for the two 2020 E3G2 raw/calibrated pairs. The official SIS says the LUT formula converts dark-subtracted raw counts to radiance in mW, followed by destriping and post-processing (including keystone and radiance adjustments). Therefore, the measured LUT-stage to archived-Level-1 ratios are stage-to-stage diagnostics. They do not by themselves establish a LUT scale error. No mask was assigned; M2 remains blocked.

**Source data:** Paired observations `20200208T0057589143_m65` and `20200208T1245274109_d18`; official E3G2 validity, saturation, and LUT tables in `data/pradan/iir.zip`. Source archive SHA-256 and QUB MD5 values are retained in the [summary JSON](metrics/e3g2_raw_lut_calibrated_summary.json). Calibrated product units differ: `m65` is mW/cm²/sr/µm; `d18` is µW/cm²/sr/µm. The official LUT is interpreted in mW per the SIS.

**Method:** Run `uv run --no-project --with numpy --with matplotlib python scripts/audit_e3g2_raw_lut_calibrated_pairs.py`. The script mapped raw and calibrated cubes using their inventory labels and scanned all 127 E3G2-valid bands and every spatial pixel for finite/zero/negative values, raw counts at or above `Dynamic_Range`, and two candidate saturation thresholds. LUT-stage comparison uses a fixed grid of every 32nd line and every fifth sample. It applies the mode-specific LUT coefficient at each sample to the paired raw count and compares this intermediate radiance with archived Level-1 radiance converted to mW. As the guide documents subsequent destriping and post-processing, this is not a like-for-like reconstruction. Candidate thresholds are not mask labels.

**Data version and code:** Full cubes from the verified local 2020-02-08 pair inventory; no train/validation/test split applies to single-scene product QA. Git base revision `304c5e8bc880d754ecab0b75983c8370b73d033e`; audit script SHA-256 `0002ec718fb2536aff52b6d9027d983fea1f4bbdee0a12b9ea4e5008f7c10db0`.

**Observations:** In both products, all values on valid bands are finite. `m65` has 2,809 zero and 26 negative band-pixels; `d18` has 3,059 zero and 26 negative band-pixels. Only one raw band-pixel in each scene reaches `Dynamic_Range`. The XML-declared saturation comparison flags 346,864,751/346,868,750 (99.99885%) `m65` valid-band pixels and 346,927,984/346,932,250 (99.99877%) `d18` pixels. The counterfactual numeric-mW comparison flags zero in either cube. Maximum raw counts on valid bands are 9,533 (`m65`) and 9,367 (`d18`).

On the fixed grid, the median of per-band archived-Level-1/LUT-stage median ratios is 0.000200 for `m65` and 0.0001999 for `d18`; per-band ratios range from about 0.000105 to 0.00414. Median per-band correlations are 0.898 and 0.924, respectively. The V6 guide says destriping and radiance post-processing follow the LUT and attributes differences from direct raw/LUT calculations to those steps. This documents a general cause for a stage difference, but does not quantify the measured ratios or reproduce these products. A controlled reconstruction or provider clarification is still needed. See the [per-band values](metrics/e3g2_raw_lut_calibrated_by_band.csv), [JSON summary](metrics/e3g2_raw_lut_calibrated_summary.json), and [figure](figures/e3g2_raw_lut_calibrated_diagnostics.png).

**Limitations:** `Dynamic_Range` and saturation comparisons remain diagnostic candidates. The comparison omits destriping and subsequent radiance post-processing described in the official guide; it is not an independent calibration validation or a complete reproduction. Finite, zero, and negative values are not classified as valid or fill. No reflectance, thermal correction, or scientific retrieval was computed.

**Next gate:** Use a small, line- and sample-aligned E3G2 subset to verify LUT indexing and the raw count input, then reproduce available destriping/post-processing steps if their coefficients are archived. Quantify the residual against Level 1 before interpreting the scale difference. Preserve separate `m65` and `d18` unit prefixes. Keep saturation/fill semantics, independent radiance validation, and M2 blocked.

## Update 2026-10-10: exposure-scaling explanation

The physical unit test showed that both E3G2 Level-1 products are physically plausible ([unit test](../m2-radiance-unit-physics/summary.md)). The official LUT tables differ in kind:

- E1G2 holds absolute gains (median 0.0028 mW/cm²/sr/µm per count, offset 0).
- E2G2 and E3G2 hold factors near 1 with count-like offsets (median offsets 16.5 and 142).
- E4G2's median factor is exactly 1 with offset 0.

E1G2 uses a 1 ms exposure, and these E3G2 products use 10 ms. A per-band least-squares fit of Level 1 (mW) against the E3G2 LUT-stage value gives a slope of 0.000203. The E1G2 gain divided by the exposure ratio of 10 predicts 0.000220. **The median fitted-to-predicted ratio is 0.944 for both `m65` and `d18`**, with p05–p95 of 0.50–0.97 and median correlation 0.90 and 0.92. This explains the earlier "~0.0002" ratio to within about 6% at the median: the E3G2 table behaves like a count-domain correction, and absolute scale follows the E1G2 gain scaled by exposure. It is a fitted hypothesis, not a provider statement, and the low-ratio tail of bands is unexplained.
