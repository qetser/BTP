# Findings

- **IIRS 4.5–4.9 µm radiance gives physically sensible surface temperatures.** The median is 327.8 K, within about ±15 K of radiative equilibrium depending on the albedo law. The independent topography test (r = 0.88 against SLDEM local illumination) confirms that the signal is surface emission.
- **Model offset depends on the albedo law.** With a constant A = 0.12, the median residual is −1.7 K (topography module). With the Hayne et al. (2017) incidence-dependent albedo, the computed mean residual is +8 to +18 K, depending on A0 (see the last finding). The defensible statement is agreement within about ±15 K. Morning thermal lag (cooler) and roughness (warmer at nadir) act in opposite directions. Separating them needs Diviner data at matched local time (about 08:15).
- **Albedo pattern.** Dark units come closest to the equilibrium model, and the bright highland is coolest relative to it. The model uses a single albedo of 0.12, so the residual tracks albedo as expected. This supports a physical signal rather than an instrument artifact.
- **The reflected term matters at a small level.** It is about 6% of the 4.7 µm radiance and biases temperature by about +2 K if neglected, as in Chauhan et al. (2021) and CH2IIRS. This pipeline removes it.
- **Failed gate: exploratory 3 µm band depth.** The pixel-scale spread (p05–p95: −0.15 to 0.17) exceeds the median (0.045). A 3.4 µm kink remains after thermal removal. Do not report hydration from this product.
- **Open question:** Laferriere and Bramson (LPSC 2025, 1644) found Level-1 differences near 3 µm and beyond 4 µm after raw-count recalibration. The magnitude of that effect on this scene is unknown.
- **Open: absolute temperature level (2026-10-10).** Against the Hayne et al. (2017) incidence-dependent albedo law, IIRS is 8–18 K warmer than radiative equilibrium. Against a constant A = 0.12, it is −1.3 K. Roughness or a radiance bias beyond 4 µm could explain the offset. Treat absolute temperature as uncertain at the ±15 K level until a Diviner comparison is done. Relative temperature patterns are robust (r = 0.877 with terrain illumination).
