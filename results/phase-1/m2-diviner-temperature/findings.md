# Findings

- **IIRS is warmer than Diviner bolometric temperature by a median of +5.0 K** at 08:07–08:15 local time (33 bins, IQR +3.4 to +7.1 K). On an emissivity-1 basis the offset is +3.2 K. The sign matches the expected roughness effect at 4.7 µm, and the size lies within the 0 to about +12 K expected range. [Values](metrics/e1g2_iirs_vs_diviner_summary.json)
- **The latitude profiles agree (r = 0.81).** Both instruments show the 18°N Montes Apenninus minimum at 311 K and cooling north of 20°N. This confirms that the IIRS relative temperature pattern is real at 1° scale.
- **The earlier constant-albedo agreement was partly coincidental.** Diviner is below the constant-albedo equilibrium model in every bin (median 7.4 K, range 2.5–16.9 K, computed from the two saved tables). IIRS sits about 5 K closer to that model, which is why the constant-albedo fit looked good. This matches the morning thermal lag noted in the thermal module.
- **Open: the offset depends on albedo.** Dark mare bins give +6.5 to +10.6 K, and bright highland bins give +0.3 to +1.6 K (r = −0.57 against 1.5 µm reflectance). Candidate causes are an error in the reflected-term removal, emissivity that varies with composition near 4.7 µm, a radiance offset in IIRS Level 1, or stronger roughness effects on darker, warmer surfaces. This module does not separate them.
- **Interpolation limit.** The GCP has almost no data at 7.88 h and 8.12 h along this longitude, so every bin interpolates between 7.62 h and 8.38 h. Single-cell values near 08:00 can differ by up to 13 K between neighbouring local-time bins. Bin-level pooling reduces this, but the per-bin result carries a few kelvin of uncertainty.
- No quality gate failed. The IIRS per-bin medians reproduce the saved thermal-module table to within 0.05 K.
