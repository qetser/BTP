# Initial IIRS evidence and gap matrix

**Status:** Initial targeted literature review, 2026-09-28. This is not a systematic review and does not establish a novelty claim.

| Topic | Published or official evidence | Current project evidence | Remaining gap and next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product levels and lineage | PRADAN describes separate raw and calibrated IIRS products and says the PDS4 XML labels contain product details. The supplied IIRS User Guide V6 explains `nri` raw, `nci` calibrated, and Level 1 apparent-reflectance conversion. | Two raw/calibrated pairs from 2020-02-08 and the 2026-02-27 E1G2 pair match on timestamp, job ID, orbit, dimensions, and wavelength table. The E1G2 pair's ZIP CRCs and embedded cube MD5 values pass. The official SIS identifies calibrated products as Level 1 radiance. A fixed-grid comparison to the official LUT gives a median calibrated/LUT ratio of 1.00375 and median per-band correlation of 0.922 after the SIS unit conversion. This supports internal scale consistency, not independent calibration validation. The official saturation XML declares radiance units of µW/cm**2/sr/µm and dynamic range in counts. Applying those fields flags 99.878921% of valid-band calibrated values, while no paired raw counts reach `dynamic_range`. | Declared units are known, but field application and the observed mismatch remain unresolved. Do not make masks from these comparisons. The calibrated label declares no missing-value constant. Obtain authoritative mask and fill rules, validate radiance against an independent reference and register geometry against an independent lunar reference, then select independent coverage before M2/M3. |
| Thermal-capable mode | Verma et al. (2022) used E1G2 Level 1 radiance and 2.5–5 µm data for thermal correction. The 2026 reflectance sensitivity study uses 4504–4875 nm as a longwave temperature interval. | The official local mode workbook marks E1G2 bands 226–248, centered from 4504.1 to 4874.9 nm, as valid. Current E3G2 pilot validity stops at 3155.9 nm. The matched E1G2 scene is acquired. Its LUT comparison supports internal scale consistency, but applying the saturation threshold under its declared units conflicts with observed values. | Keep the E1G2 mode as a candidate, not a validated M2 scene. Resolve threshold application and fill rules, validate radiance independently, and register geometry against a reference before thermal correction. |
| Temperature and reflectance uncertainty | Gou et al. (2026) report a more stable temperature estimate from 4504–4875 nm than from 2566–2937 nm. Their sensitivity example gives a 14.39% reflectance difference at 3156 nm for a 4 K temperature difference. | No temperature or reflectance product has been computed locally. | Propagate temperature uncertainty into corrected reflectance and 3 µm band depth. Test fixed emissivity assumptions against a documented range and reference products. |
| Thermal correction and 3 µm hydration | Chauhan et al. (2021) used co-located temperature measurements for physics-based thermal correction. Verma et al. (2022) used an extended IIRS spectral range and fixed emissivity. | Current pilots have no validated longwave thermal mask and remain Level 1 radiance. | Reproduce an established method on E1G2, preserve pre/post correction spectra, and compare temperature and spectral residuals with independent references. Do not infer water ice from a 3 µm feature alone. |
| Apparent and photometrically corrected reflectance | Chauhan et al. (2025) describe thermal correction, apparent reflectance, empirical-line adjustment, standard geometry, a 0.8–3.3 µm product, and comparison with same-region M³. Bose et al. (2024) evaluate an empirical lunar photometric correction over 0.8–2.0 µm and compare with M³. | No reflectance product or M³ overlap analysis exists locally. | Obtain overlapping M³ data. Document response matching, registration, geometry, and uncertainty. Treat the 2024 shortwave photometric result as complementary evidence, not validation of 3 µm thermal correction. |
| Mineral labels and held-out evaluation | The USGS unified lunar map supplies geologic units at 1:5,000,000 scale. It does not provide per-pixel mineral abundance labels. | No reference data or reviewed labels have been acquired. The three current strips are low-latitude and insufficient for independent evaluation. | Acquire M³ L2, USGS geology, and suitable spectral references. Review labels, exclude boundaries, and freeze splits by nonoverlapping orbit or province before training. |
| Public processing implementation | The public CH2IIRS repository links its thermal and phase correction tools to the 2022 and 2025 papers and is licensed under GPL-3.0. | The repository has not been downloaded or executed in this project. | Use it as an audit reference only. Record its revision and license before any reuse. Independently inspect units, masks, assumptions, constants, and implementation behavior before considering reproduction. |

## Current priority

Resolve how the E1G2 saturation-radiance and raw-count dynamic-range fields should define masks, and obtain documented fill/per-pixel validity rules before correction. The official XML declares the field units explicitly, but under those units the radiance threshold flags almost all values in valid bands while paired raw counts do not reach the table's dynamic-range field. Neither comparison defines a mask. The calibrated values agree with the official LUT internally after the SIS unit conversion, but independent calibration validation remains necessary. Register the sparse geometry against an independent lunar reference and measure residuals, then run a one-scene thermal-correction pilot with uncertainty checks and independent spectral comparisons. Continue M1 coverage selection in parallel, but freeze no M3 split until footprints and labels are reviewed.
